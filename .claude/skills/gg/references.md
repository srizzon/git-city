# Trailer references

Famous launch trailers broken down shot by shot, and what trailer editors say about music and sound. Made for the Git City Towns launch (2026-09-28); reuse it for any film.

How it was done: the four trailers marked "watched" were downloaded (yt-dlp), cut into frames at 2 per second (ffmpeg), read frame by frame, with their captions/transcripts and a loudness curve (EBU R128 momentary loudness). The rest come from write-ups, ad databases and wikis (timecodes approximate).

## The two lessons that changed our script
1. **The good ones have a hero and a story, not a montage.** Fall Guys is Rocky: warm-up, fall, suit-up in the dark, climb, near-fall, crown. Clash is a grudge: raided, "Revenge", payoff.
2. **The game's own UI is the caption.** "Who Is The Impostor?", "FINAL ROUND", "WINNER", "Your Defense Lost · Revenge". No explaining cards.

## From write-ups (approximate)
- **Brawl Stars — "No Time to Explain" (80s):** Colt keeps trying to explain and chaos keeps interrupting him. Refusing to explain is the pitch. Quiet intro, everything drops in at 7.5s.
- **Clash of Clans — "Ride of the Hog Riders":** one Hog Rider calls for backup; his doppelgängers drop their hobbies (arm-wrestling, a bath) and charge. The build is funny because the payoff is obvious.
- **Clash Royale — "A Most Ridiculous Duel":** each spot teaches one fake "Rule of the Duel"; stadium-rock on a pocket duel.
- **Splatoon Splatfest:** the hosts ARE the teams (Pearl vs Marina); results revealed category by category, upsets possible.
- **Rocket League F2P (103s, CG):** orange vs blue face-off, blackout rebirth at ~31s, goal explosion; tagline = the player's verb ("TAKE YOUR SHOT") then date.
- **Fortnite Chapter 2:** black hole → one boot in a puddle: huge-to-human match cut. Features shown as actions, never explained.
- **Apple "Get a Mac":** both sides named in 3s in an identical frame; one line repeated three times decides the matchup; silent product end card.
- **Pokémon GO reveal:** ends on a crowd winning together in a real place (Times Square), not one hero.
- **Balatro:** the scoring number counts up on screen the whole time; passing the target bursts into flame. The rule is a number you watch.

## Music and sound
- **Trailer music needs build, variety and finality** (Derek Lieu, "Developing an Ear for Trailer Music"): end more intense than it began; instruments entering and leaving; a real ending, never a fade or abrupt stop. Combine 2–3 cues if one track doesn't build. Mark the song's section changes and cut picture to them.
- **Loudness stays high almost the whole way, with 1–3 drop-outs at most, each on a story hinge** (measured above: Fall Guys 20.5s, Clash 11s and 43–48s, Brawl Stars intro until 7.5s).
- **Game sounds, not generic trailer sounds** ("Targeting Your Audience With the Trailer's Sound Effects"): the memorable sound is the game's own (Among Us klaxon). Put it alone, on a black or in a music drop-out, so it can't be missed.
- **Contrast works:** a sweet song over chaos (Fall Guys).
- **Music-video cutting** only once the footage is clear; clarity first.

## Patterns to reuse
1. A hero to root for, and a turn: loses → suits up → climbs → nearly loses → wins.
2. The game's UI as the only caption; a number that changes between beats as the spine.
3. Open in the action; stillness/black before the climax; the crown lands on the hero.
4. A button that is a joke or a loop, then the verb + availability ("JUMP IN NOW!", "Available later today").
5. Name the enemy; let the sides speak.
